Quarter to eight in the morning, in Walt's dining room. Kambria was still in her agency fleece from an all-night run, and the blood-pressure cuff was on his arm.

"One forty over eighty-eight," she said. "You've been drinking coffee."

"I've been drinking coffee since Nixon."

"Nixon didn't have your arteries." She turned the Journal around, read the article about Adrian Crowe and the verse he had written beside it in red, and clipped it with his scissors. "You marked Daniel nine, twenty-seven, next to a banker."

"All my life I've read that Book," Walt said. "Every morning, right here, the paper next to it. Kings and battles. Babylon and Persia. Things that happened a long time ago to people who weren't me. I used to read the Bible like history, Kambria. Now it reads like the morning paper."

"Walt. Do you pray about it?"

He picked up the red pen and set it down. "I read it."

"That's not what I asked you."

"The Lord knows what He's doing. He doesn't need an old man telling Him how to run things."

"It isn't telling Him anything. It's talking to Him. You've been reading His mail forty years and never once written back."

Walt folded the paper in thirds, the edges squared. "You'll be late getting to bed. You've been up all night."

At the door she turned. "I'm praying for you, Walt. Every night. You can't stop me."

"Never tried."

"I noticed."
