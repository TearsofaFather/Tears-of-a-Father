The face of the image hung over the plaza. At the foot of it, narrow and pale in black, Vale was waiting.

"You're the carpenter."

"I'm the carpenter."

"All this time. I could have taken you any night. I let you run because you were gathering them for me. Then I let you keep talking, because a voice in the dark gives people something to be against. You were the most useful enemy I ever had."

"Maybe. Or maybe you kept me alive because God wasn't done with me. You don't know which. Neither do I. That's His to know."

"There is one thing I never learned. I have your cellars, your attics, your false walls. A box of white stones I cannot read. Most of your people go somewhere, and I have never once found where. Tell me, and every one of the others walks home tonight."

"You wouldn't keep that."

"Perhaps not."

"And I wouldn't tell you if you would. I couldn't draw it for you if I tried. You'd walk past the door in daylight."

"That sounds like a verse."

"It's a promise. Psalm thirty-one. Thou shalt hide them in the secret of thy presence from the pride of man."

"I knew your name. Your son's. The name of the song. I let it play over my kingdom every night. A boy playing for a father who won't come. I understood that song better than you think."

"It was my son's song for me. It was never mine. The tears in it are His."

Vale's voice dropped. "A cell, the morning of the twenty-sixth. A woman in chains. The chains locked around nothing. Tell me what happened to her."

"She was caught up."

"Where?"

"To meet the Lord in the air. For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout."

For a second nothing moved in his face. "I do not believe in your God."

"I know."
